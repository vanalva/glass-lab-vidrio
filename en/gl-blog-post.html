How laminated glass transforms residential architecture

15 MAR 2026 MATERIAL 5 MIN

Laminated glass has stopped being a purely technical solution and become an expressive resource within contemporary architecture. In Panama's tropical context, where light is intense and the indoor-outdoor relationship defines the experience of a space, this material offers a rare combination of safety, solar control and aesthetic possibility.

Unlike tempered glass, which shatters into small pieces, laminated glass keeps its structural integrity thanks to the PVB or SGP interlayer bonding the sheets together. That property makes it the preferred option wherever safety comes first: balustrades, façades, skylights and large-format doors.

The invisible layer that changes everything

The interlayer, that plastic film trapped between the two glass sheets, is where the real innovation happens. PVB (polyvinyl butyral) interlayers offer excellent adhesion and acoustic properties, while SGP (SentryGlas Plus) provides five times the structural stiffness, allowing larger panels at a lower thickness.

In recent projects we have worked with coloured, translucent and metallic interlayers that transform how the glass reads without giving up its technical properties. An iridescent interlayer, for instance, can create colour effects that shift with the viewing angle, adding an aesthetic dimension no other material can reach.

Laminated glass is not only a technical decision — it is a statement about how we want light to inhabit a space.

In a tropical climate, laminated glass with a solar-control interlayer cuts heat transmission without darkening the space. Combined with a low-emissivity coating, the result is a panel that filters up to 70% of solar energy while keeping visual transparency almost intact. The interior stays bright but comfortable, with no need for curtains or blinds that would break the visual connection to the outside.

For architects looking to take their residential projects further, laminated glass is a chance to work with a material that is structure, surface and light all at once.
